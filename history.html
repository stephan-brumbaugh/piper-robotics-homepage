FRC 1802 · FIRST Robotics Competition

Team Stealth

Tempus Fugit · Time flies

Piper's original team. Team Stealth was formed in September 2005 and has built a full-size competition robot every season since 2006. Over the years the team has been supported by the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation (founding sponsor), Black & Veatch, JCPenney, Northrop Grumman, Honeywell, Kastle Grinding, KC STEM Alliance, The Boeing Company, the Piper Robotics Alumni Foundation and many local businesses.

“To encourage students and mentors alike to integrate science and technology into everyday situations, while challenging themselves in an innovative and safe environment. To push team members out of their comfort zones and to experience previously unattainable levels of success. To provide a working atmosphere that incorporates fun, education, gracious professionalism, and team-building opportunities.”

Team Stealth mission statement. Core values: Teamwork · Student Driven · Innovative Engineering · Community Outreach · Oomph! By 2025 the team summed up its values as family, quality and innovation.

It started in August 2005, when senior Evan Johnson wanted to take AP Physics and Piper didn't offer it. Physics and anatomy teacher Dr. Becky Knetter ("Dr. K") went looking for other ways to challenge students and found FIRST. That September she registered Team 1802 with 12 students, 6 mentors and a one-year $18,000 Kauffman Foundation grant, and FRC 1108 Panther Robotics from Paola High School mentored the rookies. In October the students chose the name Stealth, the motto Tempus Fugit, and orange and black as team colors instead of the school's purple, black and white. They named the rookie robot Knight Hawk. At kickoff in January 2006, four mentors traveled to New Hampshire while students watched the reveal on the NASA website. The team built a defensive robot that picked up balls and scored in the low goals, then road-tripped to Denver because Kansas City had no regional yet. Along the way, team member Mo pulled out his prayer mat at every stop and prayed for the team.

In August 2006, membership opened to the whole school: 17 students, only 4 of them returning after most of the first team graduated. The team worked to find new funding before build. The robot placed tubes on the bottom and middle rows of the rack and could lift two alliance partners for bonus points. For Dean Kamen's "homework" to spread the word about FIRST, students wrote to senators, congressmen and businesses, and several local and state officials came to watch. The team launched its first website, built on WordPress with a news blog, in February. In March it competed at the very first Greater Kansas City Regional, at Hale Arena with 50 teams, and was picked for an alliance that lost in the semifinals to the eventual champions. That July, at the week-long Wyandotte County Fair demo, the mayor and a city commissioner each took the controls and raced to score a tube. In August 2007 the team spent the new school year recruiting and doing outreach demos.

The team's best build season yet: most of the robot, which raced the track, knocked balls off the overpass and launched them back over it, was done in the first three weeks, leaving three weeks to tune it while the team kept finding sponsors and doing demos. At the regional the team practiced and taught safety, and its safety captain was named Safety Captain of the Day. The 10th-ranked Team Stealth was picked by the 8th alliance and became the Cinderella alliance that won the Regional Championship, sending Piper to its first FIRST Championship in Atlanta. In May, founding coach Dr. Knetter retired; she had built the team and inspired every student who came through her classroom. With no school coach, students still ran summer demos at the WYCO Fair. In September, history teacher Ron Brown stepped up to coach. In November the team made the semifinals at the first-ever Cowtown ThrowDown, an off-season event hosted by Team Driven 1730 and Cerner.

LUNACY celebrated the moon landing with a low-traction floor that made robots slide like they were on ice. The team built at the Treff family's home, and the robot collected Moon Rocks and shot them into the trailers on opponents' robots. The regional didn't go as well as hoped, but the team won the Website Excellence Award for its website. Coach Ron Brown left Piper in May after one season, but students kept meeting and ran the annual garage sale and WYCO Fair demo on their own. Chemistry teacher Eric Mabie became coach in September. At Cowtown in November, the team ran a redesigned robot to the semifinals and won Best Dressed for its pit shirts.

BREAKAWAY was robot soccer on a field split by two big bumps, so the team built a suspension to drive over them and a pneumatic kicker. In February, just before his first regional as coach, Eric Mabie, an officer in the Missouri National Guard, was deployed overseas for the second time since he started teaching at Piper. Dr. Knetter came back to lead the team at the regional. The robot wasn't picked for the playoffs, but the team lent its driver station laptop and helped other teams finish their robots, which earned the Gracious Professionalism Award. That summer the team kept up its usual fundraisers and demos.

A 12-member team with a five-person executive board, 2 coaches and 4 mentors, meeting Monday through Saturday in build season. The team moved from an 820 sq ft shop into a new 3,445 sq ft shop and helped launch Piper's high school robotics lab. Over three years its demos at Science City reached more than 10,000 people; it hosted Girl Scout Days for Fort Leavenworth troops, helped Teams 1710 and 5268 donate 200+ LEGO sets to Children's Mercy Hospital, and hosted the FTC KC West League Tournament two years running (31 teams each time). Because school rules ended sponsor runs, the Piper Robotics Alumni Foundation was formed to help fund the program. The team fundraises for all of Piper Robotics, and its core values are family, quality and innovation. The robot averaged about 103 points per qualification match at the state championship.

From the team website · 2014–2015

How Team Stealth ran [3][r]

Around 2014–2015, the team website described how the program worked day to day. Here is what it said.

About the team

FIRST Team Stealth is a high school robotics team that participates in the FIRST Robotics Competition. What started as just an idea in a physics student's mind quickly grew into a team that competes every year against teams from across the globe, in a friendly, life-changing competition built around Gracious Professionalism. Students learn to use tools from screwdrivers and hammers to table saws and drills, but before they use any of them, they learn safety first.

Build season

Build season was a six-week stretch from early January to mid-February. Mentors guided students as they voted on a design that fit the year's game and then built it.

  • Weekdays: 3–6 PM and 6–9 PM shifts
  • Saturdays: 9 AM–12 PM and 12–3 PM shifts
  • Off-season: Tuesday and Thursday evenings at Piper High School

The robotics class

Students and the coach persuaded the Piper High principal to start a robotics class. Students learned to design robots efficiently, sketching robots to fit set parameters and then creating final designs with components and wiring. Projects included building a working robot arm and claw from materials at home, like cardboard, rubber bands and glue.

Team operations

  • Co-CEOs kept the team organized and efficient and ran the Executive Board.
  • Build Director oversaw design and production of the robot and anything else the team built.
  • Financial Director built and followed the budget, organized fundraisers and worked with PR on sponsorships.
  • Safety Captain kept team members and visitors safe, kept the team in line with UL and OSHA standards, and trained the Safety Cadet.
  • Safety Cadet worked alongside the Safety Captain and filled in when needed.
  • Public Relations / Marketing Director managed the team's image, photos and video, award preparation and promotional items like buttons and wristbands.
  • Students were "the heart and soul of Team Stealth": they planned events, prepared presentations, managed the team and built the robot.
  • Mentors offered guidance, wisdom, education and friendship, asking questions to help students find their own solutions.

The Piper Craft Show

The team hosted three craft shows a year, in spring, fall and winter, at Piper High School and Piper Elementary. With about 100 vendors per show, they brought in most of the money for build season. Vendors sold everything from candles and scarves to plants and jewelry, and each show held a raffle for a prize basket worth about $500. The Winter show was the biggest: in 2013 it set a record with more than 90 vendors, and the Piper Booster Club hosted Breakfast with Santa. Booths were 8 by 10 feet for $35, plus $10 for electricity, and students helped vendors load and unload. Contact: Assistant Coach Karen Treff, pipercraftshow@gmail.com. The Spring show was held the first Saturday of May, 8 AM–3 PM.

The annual garage sale

Every year students canvassed garage, yard and estate sales around Kansas City and asked to take the leftovers, then sold them at the team's own sale. It was the team's biggest single fundraiser, bringing in over $3,000 a year for building materials and regional registration. The 2014 sale took donations after May 4 at 13598 Donahoo Rd, Kansas City, KS 66109 (contact: teamstealth1802@gmail.com).

Joining the team

  • Scholarships: as of January 2015, over 175 FIRST scholarship providers offered more than 1,000 scholarships worth over $20 million.
  • Engineering experience: "As long as you can add, subtract, multiply, divide, and are willing to get your hands a little dirty, anyone can build a robot."
  • Volunteering: students earned the 40 volunteer hours needed for National Honor Society by working football and basketball concession stands, the three craft shows and other community events.
  • Lettering: students who kept at least 70% of required build-season hours could letter in FIRST Team Stealth.

Sponsorship (2013–14 sponsor letter)

In 2013, competition costs topped $12,000, with $5,000 for registration and the kit of parts alone. The team asked local businesses for donations and offered to demonstrate the robot for them.

Sponsor tiers: $1–249, name or logo in the team pamphlet · $250–459, logo on team shirts and banner · $500–999, logo on shirt, robot and banner · $1,000–1,999, logo on shirt, double space on robot, and banners · $2,000–7,000, logo and name on shirt, robot, banner and promotional items. Contact: Eric Mabie, 913-721-2100, emabie@piperschools.com.

Weekly blurb to parents, Jan 11, 2015

"Hey Parents and Guardians! As you hopefully have heard on January 3rd we had our yearly kickoff in which the team learned what this year's event is: Recycle Rush. Since kickoff the team has come together and brainstormed several different ideas and designs for our next robot. This week we have begun building our Frankenbot, and with hard work and cooperation we are very close to being able to drive our Frankenbot, and then we will be so much closer to our final product. Thank you for taking the time to read our weekly blurb, and remember to talk to your kids and have them keep you updated on what's going on in the shop."

The website itself

The team's first website launched in February 2007 on WordPress, with a news blog. Team websites won awards in 2009 and 2011. The 2014 site was built by students, with Aiden Webb (class of 2013) as administrator. It had a members area with a time clock, calendar and photo gallery, a blog, and a "gameARCADE" of HTML5 games the web team made, Stealth Brick Breaker and Stealth Flyer. The team was also on Facebook, Twitter (@TeamStealth1802) and Vimeo.
